Legitimate Gift Card Promotions: A Practical Safety Checklist

Gift card offers can be discounts, loyalty rewards, sweepstakes, or sponsored sign-up campaigns. They do not all work the same way. Use this guide to understand the offer type and check the rules before sharing personal information.

Updated November 16, 2026 · Independent consumer information from Gift Drop USA

Our standard: A promotion should clearly identify its operator, explain what a participant must do, describe the reward and its conditions, and provide a privacy notice. A familiar brand logo or an attractive dollar amount is not enough to establish legitimacy.

Four common types of gift card promotions

Offer typeHow it usually worksWhat to verify
Retail discountYou buy a gift card for less than its face value or receive a bonus with a purchase.Merchant, expiry, exclusions, purchase minimum, and refund policy.
Loyalty rewardYou earn points or a gift card after qualifying purchases or activity.Point value, qualifying activity, redemption threshold, and expiration.
Sweepstakes or giveawayYou enter for a chance to be selected; winning is not guaranteed.Official rules, sponsor, entry period, odds information where required, and prize delivery terms.
Sponsored sign-up or lead offerYou submit details or complete actions for a third-party advertiser or sponsor.Data sharing, marketing consent, all required steps, sponsor identity, and whether a reward is guaranteed or conditional.

Use this seven-point verification checklist

  1. Identify the legal sponsor. Find the company responsible for the promotion. Search its name independently and compare the website domain with its official contact information.
  2. Find the full rules before entering. Look for eligibility by location and age, dates, limits, selection criteria, reward value, fulfillment timing, and exclusions.
  3. Know exactly what you are getting. A gift card, PayPal balance, discount code, cash payment, and chance to win are not interchangeable. Check the precise reward format.
  4. Understand every required action. If a promotion asks for an email, ZIP code, survey, trial, purchase, or subscription, determine whether each step is optional or mandatory and whether any later cost applies.
  5. Read the privacy policy and consent boxes. Understand whether information is shared with sponsors or marketing partners, how contact may occur, and how to withdraw consent.
  6. Reject pay-to-claim demands. Do not send money, gift cards, cryptocurrency, or a transfer to unlock a prize. The FTC says legitimate prizes are free to collect.
  7. Protect account access. Never give a promotion your PayPal or bank password, one-time passcode, Cash App PIN, or full card security code.

Why "free" does not always mean "no conditions"

Some legitimate marketing campaigns ask people to complete a sign-up or other qualifying action. That does not automatically make the campaign a giveaway, and it does not mean a reward is guaranteed. Read the terms to see whether the action is simply an entry, a lead submission, a purchase, a trial, or a sequence of tasks.

For email-and-ZIP offers, pay particular attention to marketing consent and data sharing. You may receive follow-up messages or be contacted by partners. If the operator does not explain this clearly, do not continue.

How to research a promotion independently

  • Search the sponsor’s exact business name together with words such as "complaint", "terms", and "privacy".
  • Open the sponsor’s official website by typing its address yourself rather than trusting a look-alike link.
  • Check the date and availability of the offer. Expired promotions should not be presented as active.
  • Keep a copy of the terms and confirmation screen if you decide to participate.
  • Report suspected prize fraud to the Federal Trade Commission.

Frequently asked questions

Can a legitimate promotion ask for my email address?

Some marketing offers request an email address, but you should understand the purpose, consent, data sharing, and follow-up contact before submitting it. An email request alone does not prove that an offer is legitimate.

Should I pay a fee to receive a gift card prize?

Do not pay an upfront fee to claim a prize. Requests for processing fees, taxes, gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency are major warning signs. Check the FTC guidance before taking action.

Does this checklist certify a specific offer?

No. It is a general evaluation guide, not a certification or endorsement. Every promotion must be checked against its own sponsor, rules, and privacy policy.

Bottom line: Verify the operator, read the terms, understand the data request, and never pay to claim a supposed prize. When essential details are hidden, walk away.
